The ordering balances durable reputation, reach, direct technical contribution,

* 1. Steve Yegge

#+REVEAL_HTML: <div style="font-size: 0.80em;">

#+BEGIN_QUOTE
“Your work isn’t hard enough right now.”

“Everyone has a discernment horizon.”

“Superhuman means unverifiable.”
#+END_QUOTE

#+REVEAL_HTML: </div>

*Signal:* Capability is useless beyond our ability to challenge and judge it.

#+BEGIN_NOTES

Why him: influential engineer-writer from Amazon and Google; creator of Beads
and Gas Town; currently one of the loudest advocates for multi-agent software
development and AI literacy.

All three excerpts: Steve Yegge, “The Flat Curve Society,” 19 June 2026.
https://steve-yegge.medium.com/the-flat-curve-society-36c8b01eb33b

Context: Yegge distinguishes a demand horizon--the hardest problem a user can
pose--from a discernment horizon--the hardest answer a user can judge. This is
an argument for personal evals and against treating access to a smarter model
as an automatic productivity gain.

#+END_NOTES

* 2. Linus Torvalds

#+REVEAL_HTML: <div style="font-size: 0.82em;">

#+BEGIN_QUOTE
“AI is a great tool, but it’s a tool.”

“These days, I’m figuring out AI tools are good too.”

“AI is not changing programming.”
#+END_QUOTE

#+REVEAL_HTML: </div>

*Signal:* This is a powerful abstraction shift, not the abolition of engineering.

#+BEGIN_NOTES

Why him: creator and long-term maintainer of Linux, creator of Git, and the
most influential living example of accountable, large-scale open-source
engineering.

All three excerpts are from Torvalds's Open Source Summit North America
conversation on 20 May 2026, reported 21 May:
https://www.techradar.com/pro/ai-is-a-great-tool-but-its-a-tool-linus-torvalds-lays-out-his-complex-love-hate-relationship-with-ai

The wider context is nuanced: he compares AI with assemblers and compilers as
productivity tools, while rejecting claims that generated code removes the
human engineering role. In separate May comments, he demanded that AI-assisted
bug reports add a patch or other human value rather than flooding maintainers.

#+END_NOTES

“Pick the right model for the job.”

“A strategy that will cope with ... unpredictable changes.”

*Signal:* Optimise the system around changing models, not for one model.

#+BEGIN_NOTES

All excerpts: Martin Fowler, “Fragments: July 13,” 13 July 2026.

* 4. Kent Beck

#+REVEAL_HTML: <div style="font-size: 0.84em;">

#+BEGIN_QUOTE
“Nobody wants agents.”

“I have a system and I want it to change.”

“Outcome-orientation is the thing.”
#+END_QUOTE

#+REVEAL_HTML: </div>

*Signal:* Agent count and orchestration are implementation details; outcomes win.

#+BEGIN_NOTES

Why him: creator of Extreme Programming and TDD, co-creator of JUnit, and a
career-long influence on feedback, design, and human-scale software practice.

All excerpts: Kent Beck, “Genie Lessons: Nobody Wants Agents,” 23 April 2026.
https://newsletter.kentbeck.com/p/genie-lessons-nobody-wants-agents

Beck describes using a coordinator, implementer, and verifier, then realising
that he was carrying coordination state the system should hold. Multi-agent is
a feature; the goal is a trustworthy change to the system.

“The real product ... is shared understanding.”

“Code stops being an asset and starts acting as a cache.”
#+END_QUOTE

*Signal:* Preserve intent, behaviour, and understanding--not every implementation.

#+BEGIN_NOTES

Why her: co-founder and CTO of Honeycomb, co-author of Observability

All excerpts: Charity Majors, “AI demands more engineering discipline. Not

Majors argues that cheap regeneration relocates rigor. The durable assets must

team understanding. She is describing a possible direction, not claiming that
today's tools can safely regenerate every production system.

“Claude Code is democratizing people’s ability to write software.”

* 39 quotes / 5 signals

#+REVEAL_HTML: <div style="font-size: 0.76em; text-align: left;">

1. *Code becomes abundant.* Intent and accepted outcomes become scarce.
2. *The bottleneck migrates.* From writing to review, verification, and judgement.
3. *The harness matters.* Context, tools, feedback, constraints, and loops compound capability.
4. *Expertise amplifies.* You cannot safely delegate beyond what you can evaluate.
5. *Responsibility remains human.* Faster generation raises the cost of weak engineering discipline.

#+REVEAL_HTML: </div>

#+BEGIN_NOTES

There is disagreement about magnitude and pace. Torvalds says AI does not
change programming; Majors says code economics have turned upside down; Cherny
says the first bottleneck has effectively disappeared. Yet the operational
advice converges.

Do not optimise for generated code, agent count, or token spend. Make intent
explicit, create hard feedback, constrain the execution environment, keep the
work observable, and measure accepted outcomes. The human role moves upward
and outward, but it does not disappear.
